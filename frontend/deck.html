Why the volume stays tiny
  • An agent paying an unknown merchant has zero recourse — no chargeback, no dispute, no reversal.
  • An honest unknown merchant has no way to prove it is safe without submitting to a gatekeeper.
  • So agents only spend amounts they can afford to lose. $0.20 at a time.
And it is about to get worse
  • Only 29% of merchants say they feel very prepared for agentic commerce fraud.
  • New dispute types fit no existing reason code — agent buys the wrong thing, or exceeds its mandate.
  • Liability is genuinely unresolved: merchant, agent vendor, protocol, or user?
Solution03

Don't identify the merchant. Bound the loss.

Every other answer tries to work out who deserves trust. We removed the question. A merchant posts collateral on-chain; the router pays instantly only up to what that merchant has actually put at risk. No score, no tier, no identity, no committee.

1 · Merchant posts a bond

Collateral, not credentials

  • Merchant opens a reserve and funds it with its own tokens.
  • That reserve is the ceiling on how much can ever be paid out instantly.
  • Anyone can post. Nobody gets approved.
2 · The router splits the order

Instant + escrowed

  • New buyer → full escrow, even against a fully collateralized merchant.
  • Settled history + posted bond → instant, with no fee on the instant part.
  • The split is computed on-chain, per order.
3 · The rug costs the rugger

Reclaim, then skim

  • No delivery → after timeout the buyer reclaims the escrow.
  • The instant portion is skimmed out of the merchant's own reserve.
  • No merchant signature required. It cannot be blocked.

The loss is bounded before anyone has to detect fraud. Fake reputation cannot beat it, because there is no reputation to fake — only money the merchant already staked.

Demo04

Watch it survive a rug, live

Five acts against real Solana devnet. Every figure is read back from on-chain accounts; every act links to the transaction on Solana Explorer.

Act 1
A merchant posts 100 tokens of collateral; a fresh buyer registers with zero settled orders.
Act 2
First order, 30 tokens → instant 0.000, escrowed 30.000. Full protection despite a fully funded merchant, because standing is still zero.
Act 3
Same buyer, next order, 60 tokens → instant 60.000, escrowed 0.000. Paid in full before any confirmation, no fee on the instant portion.
Act 4
A second merchant posts 40, takes a 40-token instant order — and vanishes. This is the rug.
Act 5
Buyer recovers 40.000 — the entire order, instant portion included — straight out of the rugger's reserve. The rugger's balance goes to 0.000. No merchant signature anywhere in that transaction.

Traction & business model05

Built, deployed, and callable by any agent

What exists today
  • Anchor program live on devnet — nine instructions covering reserves, routing, escrow, claim, dispute and timeout reclaim.
  • 14/14 tests passing, including the adversarial paths: reclaim before expiry, double settle, wrong-token-owner, and reserve withdrawal against locked exposure.
  • Five-act live demo proving the rug case end to end on real devnet.
  • MCP server — any Claude or MCP-speaking agent can call pay_merchant and reclaim_payment as tools. The escrow is agent-native, not a dashboard.
  • Browser demo that runs the whole thing with no wallet on the visitor's side.
How we make money
  • 0.50% on the escrowed portion only. Zero fee on anything paid instantly.
  • We are paid exactly when protection is used — the fee tracks risk, like an insurance premium, not a toll.
  • As a merchant posts more collateral its orders route instant, and its fees fall to zero. The incentive points the right way.
What is not revenue
  • The skimmed reserve goes entirely to the wronged buyer. We take nothing from a rug.

Competition07

Everyone is building identity. We build recourse.

The field converged on one answer — verify the agent, then permit the payment. That reintroduces the gatekeeper crypto payments were meant to remove, and still leaves the buyer with nothing when a verified merchant simply fails to deliver.

WhoWhat they actually do The gap it leaves
SkyfireKYA — "know your agent" identity and wallets ($8.5M seed) Identity is not recourse. A verified merchant that takes the money and vanishes is still gone.
NeverminedAgent-native payments, metering, access control; escrow available for high-value work Closest to us — but escrow is a platform feature with dispute handling, not merchant capital at stake. Someone still adjudicates.
PaymanControlled banking automation with human-set spend limits Caps the buyer's spend rather than making the seller liable. A capped loss is still a loss.
Visa TAP · Mastercard Agent PayNetwork-issued agent credentials on card rails Recourse exists, but it is the chargeback — permissioned, days long, and closed to merchants without a card-network relationship.
Stripe ACP · Google AP2Open protocols for agent checkout at the processor layer Standardize how an agent pays, deliberately not who is liable when delivery fails. Complementary, not competitive.
x402 itselfThe payment rail we settle on Intentionally minimal: it moves money and stops. What happens next is our entire product.
Agent CTOSMerchant-posted collateral, on-chain routing, automatic skim on timeout The only one where the seller's own money is the guarantee — permissionless to join, enforceable without the seller's cooperation or anyone's judgement.
GTM · 3-month plan08

Earn mainnet. Don't just deploy to it.

This program holds other people's money and pays out without a human in the loop. The plan is a deliberate promotion path — devnet to testnet to mainnet — where each stage has an exit bar it has to clear before the next one starts.

Month 1 · Devnet — break it on purpose
  • Adversarial test suite: reclaim races, double-settle, reserve withdrawal mid-exposure, order-id collision, malicious token mints.
  • Fuzz the router across the full amount × standing × reserve space; assert instant + escrowed always equals the order and never exceeds the bond.
  • Soak test: thousands of orders to find PDA rent, account-size and compute-budget ceilings.
  • Exit bar: no invariant violation in 10k randomized orders.
Month 2 · Testnet — break it under load
  • Deploy to testnet and run against real validator conditions — congestion, forks, dropped blockhashes.
  • Concurrency: many buyers against one merchant's reserve at once, proving locked exposure can never be oversubscribed.
  • Economic simulation: model a rational rugger and confirm the reserve makes rugging unprofitable at every order size.
  • External review of the program and a public bug bounty on testnet funds.
  • Exit bar: clean review, no critical findings open.
Month 3 · Mainnet — small, capped, real
  • Capped beta: hard per-order and per-reserve limits in the program itself, raised only on evidence.
  • Launch with 10 real agent↔merchant pairs — the x402 service directories are where they already are.
  • Distribution is the MCP server: any Claude or agent framework adds escrowed pay as one tool, no integration project.
  • Exit bar: 10 merchants with live collateral and a real rug survived on mainnet.

The ask10

Fund the hardening, not the hype

The mechanism works — it is deployed and the rug case is proven on devnet. What it has not had is the adversarial engineering that should stand between a hackathon program and other people's money. That gap is the ask.

What we already know is unfinished — found, not guessed
  • No external audit. Every bug found so far was found by us, which is exactly the wrong sample.
  • Real bugs this build has already surfaced: accounts writable without being marked mutable (silently reverting every settlement), a buyer-standing PDA colliding across runs, merchants unable to pay their own PDA rent, transient RPC failures aborting whole runs.
  • Untested economics. The instant/escrow split and the 0.50% fee are reasoned, not simulated against a rational adversary.
  • Dispute window is unproven. claim_fulfillment and finalize_claim pass tests but have never been run adversarially at scale.
  • Concurrency is unvalidated. Locked exposure has not been tested with many buyers racing one reserve.
Where the money goes
  • Security review + bug bounty — the single largest line, and the one that gates mainnet.
  • Adversarial and economic simulation — fuzzing, invariant testing, modelling a profitable rugger.
  • The devnet → testnet → mainnet promotion on slide 8, with its exit bars.
What you get for it

A settlement-safety layer that any x402 merchant can join without permission, on the chain that already carries 65% of agent-to-agent volume — audited, capped, and live on mainnet with real collateral behind it.
